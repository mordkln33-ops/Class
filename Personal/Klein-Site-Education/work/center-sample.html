Maple Street Learning Center: a tutoring center website with online intake

An intake form and session finder that replace weeks of phone tag, with a schedule the director can update in minutes.

Client type
Tutoring center
Plan
Center
Pages
6
Timeline
4 weeks

The brief

Who
A neighborhood learning center with 8 tutors and three programs: after-school homework help, reading support for grades 2–6, and SAT/ACT prep. About 120 families enrolled. (Fictional client.)
The situation
Enrollment happens entirely by phone. The director spends weeks in phone tag with new families. The website is an aging template, the schedule is a PDF that's usually out of date, and tutors aren't mentioned at all.
Goals
Let new families enroll online. Keep the schedule current without a developer. Introduce the tutors. Help a parent find the right program in under a minute.
Constraints
The director isn't technical. Schedules change every term. Many parents use older phones. A budget of about $2,000.

The approach

Parents don't think in program names. They think "my son is falling behind in reading." So the site is organized by need first, program second.

  • Programs grouped by student need, with grade levels and times visible at a glance.
  • A three-step intake form (student, needs, availability) with a progress bar and only the essential fields required.
  • One simple schedule list the director edits in a basic content editor. The site turns it into a searchable session finder.
  • Tutor profiles with a photo, subjects and one sentence on how each tutor teaches.
  • A semester refresh plan, so new term schedules go live before families start asking.

Site structure

Home
  • Programs by need
    • Homework help
    • Reading support (grades 2–6)
    • SAT/ACT prep
  • Our tutors
  • Schedule and session finder
  • Enroll (intake form)
  • About and contact

The design

The learning tool: a reading Quick Check

A short, friendly check for grades 2–6. A student reads a few passages and answers questions, and the family sees which program fits best. The result arrives with the intake form, so the first conversation starts in the right place.

The session finder (choose a grade, subject and days to see matching sessions) is part of the website itself.

Measuring success

What I'd track in the first term:

  • Intake forms completed per month, especially on phones
  • Days from first contact to first session
  • Phone calls asking when a program is offered
