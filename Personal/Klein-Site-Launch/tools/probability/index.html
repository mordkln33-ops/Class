How it works

  1. Type one fraction. The denominator copies to the other fraction, and the second numerator fills in as the complement — the two chances always total one whole.
  2. Name both outcomes and press Build grid. Each square is one equally likely result, painted in one of two contrasting colors.
  3. Do it again on the other side for a second experiment.
  4. Press Manipulate and pick an operation. Add answers or — either one happens. Multiply answers and — both happen together.
  5. Every result is one scenario. The two spaces are independent experiments, so pairing them gives a grid of Space A across by Space B down, and every square in it is equally likely. The chances are never just added as fractions.
  6. Every square is named with its full outcome — Heads, Red · Heads, Blue · Tails, Red · Tails, Blue. The squares that do not count toward the answer have a line struck through their name.
  7. Read the blend. Squares where both chosen outcomes happen are the two colors mixed, carrying both labels separated by a comma.

Colors are re-rolled every time you build, and are always kept pale so the dark labels stay readable on a projector.
