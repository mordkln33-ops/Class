Teacher notes

Fraction bars and area models students can see

The Fraction Visualizer turns any fraction into a picture that fills half the screen. It shows equivalent fractions, adding and subtracting with a common denominator, and multiplying and dividing fractions with an area model, step by step and unsimplified, so students see where the answer comes from.

Show a single fraction

Type a numerator and denominator and press Submit. The whole is cut into equal parts and the fraction is shaded. The panel also shows the decimal, the percent and the simplified form. Fractions greater than one wrap onto a second whole, so improper fractions like 5/4 still read honestly.

Add and subtract fractions

Choose add or subtract and press Calculate. Both fractions are rewritten over a common denominator (the product of the two denominators), and the picture is drawn in two colors: the amount you started with, and the part added on or taken away. When a subtraction goes below zero, the missing part is drawn as a striped “past zero” section.

Multiply fractions with an area model

Multiplying draws a grid: the first fraction shades columns, the second shades rows, and the outlined overlap is the product. For 1/2 × 2/3, half the columns and two thirds of the rows overlap in 2 of the 6 boxes, which is 2/6, or 1/3 once simplified.

Divide fractions by multiplying by the reciprocal

When you choose divide, the second fraction flips and the operation changes to multiply right in the inputs, so the equation on the board always matches the picture. It's a natural way to show why “keep, change, flip” works.

Teaching tip: the calculator leaves its answer unsimplified on purpose. Ask the class to simplify it, then press Submit next to the big fraction to check: it simplifies, redraws in one color, and feeds the answer back into the next problem.

Frequently asked questions

How large a denominator can I use?

Denominators up to 100 work, and a picture can have up to 900 parts. That keeps every part big enough to see on a projector. Larger problems still get a numerical answer.

Does it handle negative and improper fractions?

Yes. Improper fractions wrap onto extra wholes (marked with a dashed “one whole” outline in the area model), and for negative fractions the picture shows the size, with a note that the value is negative.

Is the Fraction Visualizer free?

Yes. Every tool on MathVisuals is currently free, needs no sign-up, and runs in the browser on smartboards, Chromebooks, tablets and phones. The tool itself is copyright Mordechai Klein, so please share a link to it rather than copying it.
