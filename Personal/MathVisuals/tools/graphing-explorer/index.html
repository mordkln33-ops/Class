Teacher notes

An interactive coordinate plane for graphing lines

Graphing Explorer connects the three ways students meet a linear function: points on a coordinate plane, an equation in slope-intercept form, and a table of values. Change one, and the others update with it.

Plot ordered pairs

Click any spot where two grid lines cross to plot a point, or type an ordered pair such as (3, −4). A typed point travels from the origin, first across to its x-value and then up or down to its y-value. It shows why the x-coordinate comes first. The plane runs from −10 to 10 on both axes, covering all four quadrants.

Build a line from two points

As soon as two points aren't yet part of a line, the tool asks whether to connect them. Say yes, and the line is drawn across the whole plane with its equation in y = mx + b form and a table of values. Drag either point and the slope, intercept and table update instantly, which is a quick way to explore how slope changes.

Graph an equation

Type an equation such as y = 2x + 1, y = −3x, y = 1/2x − 3 or a vertical line like x = 4, and press Graph it. Each new line gets its own high-contrast color, and its card shows the slope, y-intercept and table.

Read the table from the graph

Hover over a line to see the ordered pair at each whole x-value. The matching row in that line's table lights up at the same time, so students see that every row of the table is a point on the line.

Projector tip: press the magnifier button in any panel's heading to pop that panel out at double size. It still works while magnified, and you can drag it by its heading to anywhere on the screen. It's ideal for zooming in on one table or the graph for the back row.

Frequently asked questions

Which equation formats does it understand?

Slope-intercept form (y = mx + b) with whole numbers, decimals or fractions for the slope and intercept, horizontal lines such as y = 5, and vertical lines such as x = −2.

How do I move or delete a point?

Drag a point to move it; it snaps to the nearest grid corner. Click a point once and press Delete point to remove it, or click its coordinates in the Points on the Graph list.

Does it work on a touchscreen or interactive whiteboard?

Yes. Points can be plotted and dragged with a finger or stylus, and all buttons are sized for touch.
