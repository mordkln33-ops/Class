Teacher notes

Build segments, perpendiculars and parallels, then measure them

Line Constructions is a set of line segments that students can build, join into shapes and measure. Make a segment a whole number of units long, join two points or midpoints, drop a perpendicular from a point to a line or copy a line in parallel, then drag the pieces together into triangles and quadrilaterals and measure every length and angle.

Make a line

The three buttons under New line each open a pop-up beside the canvas, never on top of it, and the new line shows as a dotted line until you press Create line. By length makes a segment a whole number of units long, in the middle of the canvas or, if something is already there, in the nearest free spot above it. Connect joins two points, or, given a point and a line, draws the perpendicular from the point to the line, with a right-angle mark at its foot. While it is open, the middle of every line shows as a hollow dot, so a line's midpoint can be picked like any other point: join a vertex of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side for a median, or join the midpoints of two sides for a midsegment. Parallel copies the direction of a line you pick, at whatever whole length you choose, with arrow marks showing that the two lines are parallel.

Move, turn and join

Drag the middle of a line to move it, and drag an end to turn the line about its other end. When an end touches another point, the two points snap together and become one, keeping the first point's letter. They only join if no length has to change, so a triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5 closes up exactly when the angle between the 3 and the 4 is a right angle. Joined lines move together like one shape. A line turned so that its end lands on another line near a right angle snaps perpendicular, and its length becomes the distance from its pivot to that line. A line turned close to a nearly parallel line snaps parallel. While you hold a snap, a message at the top of the canvas says what it does.

Change or delete a line

Right-click a line or an end (on a touchscreen, press and hold it) to change the line's length or color, rename the point, add the line's midpoint, continue the line past that point as a dotted line to the edge of the canvas, or open a shape at that point so the two lines come apart. A midpoint stays at the middle of its line, even when the line gets longer or shorter, and tick marks show the two equal halves. Click a line or an end without dragging to delete it.

Measure

Length and Angle under Measure work the same way: click the line, or click inside the angle near its corner, or pick it from a list written in geometry notation (AB with a bar for segment AB, ∠ABC for an angle). Each measure stays on the canvas, and updates as you move things, until you click it. Where two lines cross without a point, measuring an angle there names the crossing with the next free letter.

Projector tip: build the figure first and ask the class to predict a length or an angle before you measure it. The grid starts hidden, so students can't simply count squares; press Grid (top right) when counting units helps.

Frequently asked questions

Why are some lengths not whole numbers?

A segment made with By length is always a whole number of units. A segment that joins two points, or a perpendicular from a point to a line, is as long as the distance it covers, which is often not a whole number. That's a good moment for the Pythagorean theorem: the distance between points 3 units across and 4 units up is exactly 5.

Why didn't two ends join when I dragged them together?

Points only join when no length would change. Sliding a line never changes its length, so its end always joins a point it reaches. Turning a line only joins its end to a point that is exactly as far from the pivot as the line is long. Otherwise the line would have to stretch or shrink.

How do I build a triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5?

Make segments of 3, 4 and 5 units. Drag the 4 so that one end joins an end of the 3, then turn it until it snaps to a right angle. Drag the 5 so that one end joins the other end of the 3, and turn it until its free end meets the 4: the two ends join and the triangle closes. Try it with sides 3, 4 and 6 and the triangle never closes at a right angle.

What do the small squares, arrows and tick marks mean?

They are the usual textbook marks. A small square appears wherever two lines meet at exactly 90°. Matching arrow marks (> or >>) appear on lines made or snapped parallel to each other. Matching tick marks (| or ||) on the two halves of a line show that it has a midpoint, so the halves are equal.

How do I show that a midsegment is half the third side?

Build a triangle, open Connect and pick the hollow midpoints of two of its sides, then press Create line. Measure the new segment and the third side: it is exactly half as long. Measure the angles it makes with the other two sides and compare them with the triangle's own angles to see that it is parallel to the third side.

How do I take a shape apart again?

Right-click the point where two lines meet and choose Open the shape. The line's end becomes a separate point with its own letter and swings slightly away.

Does it work on a touchscreen or interactive whiteboard?

Yes. Drag lines with a finger or stylus. Press and hold an end for the options a right-click would open, or click a line and use the ⋯ button in the bubble that appears.
