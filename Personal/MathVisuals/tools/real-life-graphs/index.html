Teacher notes

Rate of change, slope and best-fit lines with real units

Slope is easier to understand when it has units. In Graphs in Real Life, students label the axes with real quantities, such as distance in miles and time in hours, plot data, and see the rate of change read back as “3 miles per hour,” not just “3.”

How to use it in class

  1. Name the axes. Type a label and units for each axis, for example Distance (miles) and Time (hours). The graph updates as you type.
  2. Plot the data. Click the graph to place points, or type them into the Coordinates box, like (1, 3), (2, 7), (4, 12). Each x-value can only be used once, so the data always describes a function.
  3. Choose a display. Connect the dots joins the points in order. Line of best fit draws the least-squares line and writes its equation, such as y = 2.9x + 0.4.
  4. Find the rate of change. Open the Rate of change tool and pick two points. A rise-over-run triangle appears on the graph, and the panel works through change in y ÷ change in x with the units included.

Reading the y-intercept

When the line of best fit crosses the y-axis on the graph, a labeled diamond marks the y-intercept at x = 0, with the x-axis units shown. That gives the class a concrete place to discuss starting values: “at 0 hours, the car had already gone…”

Ideas for real-world data

  • Distance traveled over time, then compare speeds of two trips
  • Cost of a phone plan or taxi ride: a starting fee plus a rate
  • Plant height over days from a class science experiment
  • Temperature over the course of a school day

Projector tip: the rate of change window can be dragged anywhere on the screen by its orange title bar, so it never covers the part of the graph you're talking about. Press Escape to close it.

Frequently asked questions

What range does the graph cover?

The x-axis runs from 0 to 30 and the y-axis from 0 to 20, which suits most first-quadrant word problems. Clicked points snap to whole numbers; typed points can be decimals.

How is the line of best fit calculated?

It uses least-squares linear regression, the same method as a graphing calculator's linear regression. It needs at least two points.

How do I delete a point?

Click the point and choose Delete this point, or remove it from the Coordinates box. Clear all removes every point.
